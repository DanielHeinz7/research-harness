`research-harness review` now ends with a "Glossary mechanics" group covering
the five mechanical checks of the glossary design: entry structure and
provenance, declared canvas coverage in elements 3, 4, 7 and 8, declared
manuscript coverage, the lexical cross-check, and accepted text stability.

Manuscript coverage resolves the frontmatter's declared list and reads the
`constructs:` declarations and glossary links in each file, covering the
paragraph each one stands in. The lexical cross-check lists a declared term or
alias that appears in a citation-carrying statement or in manuscript prose
without a resolved reference, under Unicode word boundaries and without
stemming, and it lists a label two entries claim under both rather than
picking a reading. A changed definition, origin or distinction names the
dependent canvas statements and manuscript locations and invalidates nothing;
the accepted payload is compared against the sealed decision.

The group always prints the files it read and two boundary sentences: declared
construct coverage was checked, undeclared constructs need human inspection,
and human construct judgment was not performed. A missing manuscript list, an
unsupported path and a failed read are reported as not checked, never as
clean, and a glossary that is absent or cannot be read is named as not checked
instead of reported as zero findings. The group reads only.
