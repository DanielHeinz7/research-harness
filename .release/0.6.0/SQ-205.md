The writing skills now carry the construct glossary. `canvas` says entries live
in `research/glossary.md`, that the model proposes `[proposed]` or `[tentative]`
entries with an honest `Origin:` and a resolving `Distinctions:` line, and that
it never accepts one: acceptance is the researcher's
`/research-harness:glossary-accept <id> --hash <8hex> --explained "<their
sentence>"`, and the guard denies the model the command. The model may create
the file with its `glossary-schema: 1` header; the header and the
`manuscripts:` list are the researcher's afterward. A canvas statement cites an
entry as another evidence tag, `src: glossary.md#felt-safety`; elements 3, 4, 7
and 8 declare each construct they use; a manuscript links the entry at the
definition and first substantive use and declares the rest with
`<!-- constructs: felt-safety, speaking-frequency -->`. A resolved link proves
an entry exists, not that it is accepted, right, or supporting the statement.

Where the profile says element 4 emerges from the material, a sensitizing term
may arrive now with `Definition: pending` and stays visible; no source or
definition is manufactured to make a review green, and acceptance follows
element 4's profile dependencies, so an entry whose dependencies are unmet is
refused.

`canvas-review` reports the mechanical `Glossary mechanics` group verbatim,
including its two boundary sentences, and then asks the questions the mechanics
cannot answer: whether the entries distinguish different things, whether a
definition fits this study and its source, whether the design measures the
construct the paper names, and whether an undeclared construct, a conflation or
a changed meaning slipped in. An accepted entry is the researcher's decision
about the displayed text; a `definition changed since acceptance` finding names
what rests on the entry and invalidates nothing.

Both profiles now describe the dependency enforcement `accept` performs instead
of claiming the graph is never enforced: an element may not be accepted while
an element it depends on is not, and the glossary follows element 4's edges.
What may be written, and when, is still never gated. The qualitative profile
adds the late-definition timing and keeps the glossary outside its modules —
the format is cross-genre, the timing is the profile's. The quantitative
profile says an accepted entry is a writing commitment, not registry
preregistration, and accepting one accepts no 4.1 hypothesis. `amj-map` places
the glossary in element 4 work — for the relations and mechanisms of 4.2 and
4.3 — without making the file format a Sparrowe and Mayer prescription. The
README gains a short glossary section and states the dependency check as
enforced where `accept` refuses, matching the tool.
