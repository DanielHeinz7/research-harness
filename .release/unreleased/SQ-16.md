ref: SQ-16
title: The guard that keeps commitments in the researcher's hands
bump: patch
plugins:
  - research-harness
---

One `PreToolUse` hook now stands in front of `Edit`, `Write`, `NotebookEdit`
and `Bash`. For `research/canvas.md` it reconstructs the file before and after
the write — applying an `Edit` the way the tool would — and applies four rules:
every element and question heading that was there stays; no `[accepted]`
statement appears that was not already accepted with the same text; no
`[stated]` or `[accepted]` statement is altered, removed or downgraded; and no
`[proposed]` or `[tentative]` statement is promoted to `[stated]`. Everything
else is allowed, including writing a new `[stated]` statement as a
transcription of what the researcher just said, and adding or changing the
evidence tags of a committed statement. `decisions.md` is append-only: a write
passes only when it starts with what was already there.

Shell access to the two files is read-only — `cat`, `grep`, `sed -n`,
`git diff` and the harness's own `status`, `review` and `init` subcommands.
A redirection into either file, `tee`, or an in-place `sed -i` is refused, and
`research-harness accept` is refused from the shell wherever it appears,
because running it there would be the model declaring a commitment rather than
the researcher making one.

The guard fails closed, deliberately against the fail-open doctrine the
`SessionStart` hook follows: a canvas it cannot parse, an `old_string` that is
missing or ambiguous, a notebook tool pointed at the canvas, or any error at
all ends in a denial rather than a write. Every refusal names the next legal
action — propose alongside as `[proposed]`, or let the researcher accept via
`/research-harness:accept` — so a guard that says no cannot block the harness
against itself. `canvasVerdict()` and `bashVerdict()` are exported for the
canvas server that stage 2 puts in front of the same rules.
