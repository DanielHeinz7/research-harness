SQ-29 finding F7 (lex.txt L8): `nest()` stopped at `MAX_SUBSTITUTION_DEPTH` and
returned as if the body it had not read were ordinary text. Nine nested `$( … )`
around `rm research/canvas.md` therefore reached the verdict as one opaque word
with nothing visible inside it, no word named the research state, and the removal
was allowed — the probe ran it and the canvas was gone. Seven and eight levels
still denied, because the `rm` at the bottom was lexed.

`nest()` now marks the line unreadable at the point where it stops, and
`bashVerdict` routes a line carrying that mark through the refusal it already has
for a command it could not lex at all: denied when the raw command names the
research state, allowed otherwise, with the reason naming the cause
(`… nest past the 8-deep limit …`). Because `$()`, backquotes and an unquoted
heredoc body all reach the lexer through the same `nest()`, ordinary words and
heredoc bodies are handled alike. The limit itself is unchanged.

`verification/SQ-29/lex.txt` L8 flips from ALLOW to DENY; L6 and L7 stay DENY and
every other row is unchanged. `test/guard-promotion.test.js` gains the
deep-nesting case beside the unreadable-command test, with the seven- and
eight-level controls and a nine-level line that names nothing in the state and is
still allowed.
