# Hypotheses before data

Attached to elements 4 and 6 by the `is-quantitative` profile.

For confirmatory hypothesis testing, state the theory-derived hypotheses
(element 4) and the design with its analysis plan (element 6) before inspecting
results that could influence them. Mertens & Recker (2020, HARKing discussion
and Table 2) distinguish generating a post-hoc hypothesis from presenting it as
if it were a priori, and recommend preregistering hypotheses before data
analysis. A hypothesis suggested by exploration can guide a later test; it
must be reported as exploratory for the data that suggested it. Existing or
pilot data do not make every subsequent hypothesis illegitimate (Gregor &
Klein 2014, §2.1; Mertens & Recker 2020, Table 2).

For this profile, record the hypotheses and the planned measures, sampling and
analysis choices before collecting new confirmatory data, or before analyzing
existing data for the planned test. Label any prior access to the data and
later deviations. This is the profile's documentation practice, not a claim
that the cited journals require this canvas workflow. Define constructs before
developing measures and plan their validation; preregistration does not by
itself establish measurement validity (MacKenzie et al. 2011, Figure 1).

`research-harness review` reports, under "Findings before hypotheses", when
the first log entry on element 7 has no earlier decision entry on element 4
or element 6. It blocks nothing. The check compares log sequence numbers;
it does not examine whether the hypotheses or analysis plan are complete.
An early finding can come from a pilot or exploratory study; explain that
history and separate it from the planned confirmatory test (Gregor & Klein
2014, §2.1; Mertens & Recker 2020, HARKing discussion).

This is not preregistration with a registry or results-blind peer review. The
sealed log records entry order, and the authors can publish its anchor
(`research-harness review`, "Anchor"). It cannot establish when data were
collected, first accessed or analyzed outside the log. Acceptance before a
finding was logged therefore does not prove that the hypothesis preceded
knowledge of the results. Mertens & Recker's (2020, Table 2) preregistration
and results-blind review proposals go beyond this check.
